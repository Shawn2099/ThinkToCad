# AI for Manufacturing, DFM, CAE, and Process Planning — Research Paper Catalog

## Key Question 1: Major papers on AI for manufacturing/DFM/CAE (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
There is a rapidly growing body of 2024–2026 work on LLM/multi-agent DFM redesign, AI-driven manufacturability assessment (KAN, rule+ML), AI-accelerated CAE (automated meshing, aerodynamics benchmarks), and AI-assisted process planning (semantic capability models, feature-free turning). Most are arXiv preprints; several have accepted venue placements.

### Inferences
- The 2024–2026 DFM literature is bifurcating into (a) LLM/multi-agent CAD redesign loops (Welbeck, George) and (b) parametric-feature ML manufacturability classifiers (Deylami KAN). Both avoid full CAD geometry processing.
- CAE AI work concentrates on surrogate modeling/benchmarking (aerodynamics) and automated meshing (aortic stenosis), not on LLM-driven CAE yet.
- Process planning AI on arXiv leans on symbolic/semantic capability models (Köcher) and feature-free computational geometry (Behandish), with newer LLM materials-design surveys touching planning.

### Gaps
- No arXiv paper found explicitly benchmarking aPriori/DFMPro/Fusion 360/Siemens NX DFM engines against AI models; tool comparisons are anecdotal/industry. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16586v1) notes academia-industry gap.
- Topology-optimization-specific AI-for-manufacturing papers did not surface in these arXiv queries (the "topology optimization" query returned unrelated results); coverage of TO+AI is a gap. Flagged unverified.

## Key Question 2: Methods used

### Takeaway
Methods span LLM multi-agent CAD editing, parametric-feature ML (KAN, 14 baselines), physics-informed generative models, automated meshing, feature-free computational-geometry planning, semantic capability + SMT planning, and surrogate/benchmark frameworks for CAE.

### Cited Findings
- LLM-based DFM: multi-agent review+modify with visual verification loop (Welbeck et al., arXiv:2609.05559v1); multi-agent FDM DFAM with STEP/B-Rep + GraphSAGE features (George et al., arXiv:2607.02448v2).
- Rule-based + AI: KAN manufacturability from parametric features + tolerance, vs 14 ML/DL models (Deylami et al., arXiv:2601.06334v1); X-DFS explainable AI for design-for-security (Mahfuz et al., arXiv:2411.07308v1).
- Topology optimization / inverse design: yield-optimized inverse-designed photonic EPICs with DFM (Zhou et al., arXiv:2604.15493v1); knowledge-guided inverse design perspective (Lee et al., arXiv:2506.00056v2).
- Process selection/planning: semantic capability model + SMT solver (Köcher et al., arXiv:2312.08801v2); feature-free turning planning via configuration space + AI search (Behandish et al., arXiv:1905.09434v3); situation calculus controller synthesis (De Giacomo et al., arXiv:1807.04561v1).
- Automated meshing: AI pipeline 100x faster than existing approaches for aortic stenosis hemodynamics (Ozturk et al., arXiv:2407.00535v1).
- CAE AI: automotive aerodynamics benchmarking framework with DrivAerML (Tangsali et al., arXiv:2507.10747v1); deep-learning CAD/CAE generative design of 3D wheel (Yoo et al., arXiv:2006.02138v3).

### Inferences
- "AI" in manufacturing is heterogeneous: LLM agents dominate CAD/DFM redesign; GNNs/ML classifiers dominate manufacturability; neural surrogates dominate CAE.

### Gaps
- No standardized comparison of LLM-agent DFM vs. traditional rule-based DFM (e.g., DFMPro) on identical parts; Welbeck et al. note evaluation ongoing. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05559v1)

## Key Question 3: Tools

### Takeaway
Industry DFM/CAE tools (aPriori, DFMPro, Fusion 360 Manufacturing, Siemens NX) are referenced in the broader literature (notably the CAE UX multivocal review) as the incumbent baseline that academic AI work has not yet matched in UX validation; peer-reviewed arXiv papers rarely evaluate against them directly.

### Cited Findings
- Incumbent CAE/DFM tools are industry-side; academic work focuses on technical capability without UX validation against them. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16586v1)
- LLM-for-EDA work (Xu et al., arXiv:2508.20030v1) surveys AI integration into design-to-manufacturing EDA workflows, the electronic analog of mechanical DFM tools.
- NSF AI-for-EDA workshop report (Chen et al., arXiv:2601.14541v4) lists "AI for physical synthesis and DFM" as a theme with physical manufacturing process challenges.

### Inferences
- Direct, citable head-to-head benchmarks of AI DFM vs. aPriori/DFMPro/Fusion 360/Siemens NX are not present in the arXiv record captured here; industry tooling is treated as context, not evaluated baseline.

### Gaps
- No paper in this set reports quantitative DFM-rule coverage (e.g., aPriori cost models, DFMPro rule libraries) for the AI systems. Flagged unverified.

## Key Question 4: Benchmarks or datasets

### Takeaway
Benchmarks found: CADPrompt (200 NL prompts + expert CAD code) for CAD code generation; DrivAerML for automotive aerodynamics CAE; MFCAD++ (59,665 parts) for feature recognition; synthetic 300k-design manufacturability dataset; DiFBench framework for distinctive-feature mining; MindEval2-style benchmarks are adjacent. Most DFM work uses synthetic/proprietary data.

### Cited Findings
- CADPrompt: 200 natural-language prompts paired with expert-annotated scripting code for 3D objects. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05340v2)
- DrivAerML dataset used in automotive aerodynamics AI benchmarking framework. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10747v1)
- MFCAD++: 59,665 parts used for GraphSAGE feature labeling in AgentsCAD. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02448v2)
- Synthetic dataset of 300,000 labeled designs across drilling/milling/combined for KAN manufacturability. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06334v1)
- DiFBench: configurable benchmark framework for distinctive-feature mining (10–40 docs). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00245v2)
- NSF AI-for-EDA workshop calls for robust data infrastructures for EDA/DFM AI. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14541v4)

### Inferences
- DFM datasets are mostly synthetic or small (46-part benchmark in Welbeck et al.); real industrial part libraries are not openly available.

### Gaps
- No open, standardized DFM benchmark comparable to DrivAerML for mechanical parts; Welbeck et al. 46-part benchmark is preliminary. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05559v1)
