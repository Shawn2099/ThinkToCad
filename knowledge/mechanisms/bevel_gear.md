# bevel_gear

**Type:** mechanism
**Function:** Transmit power between intersecting (usually 90deg) shafts via conical gears.

## How it works
Teeth are formed on a cone; shafts intersect at the apex. Miter gears are 1:1 bevels at 90deg. Spiral bevels give smoother/higher-speed operation. Hypoid gears have skew (non-intersecting) axes with point contact and need viscous oil.

## Key dimensions / equations
- Gear ratio T_2/T_1 = N_2/N_1 (same as spur).
- Miter: 90deg axes, 1:1 ratio.
- Spiral bevel: angled teeth for smoother engagement.
- Hypoid: offset axes; needs special lubricant.

## Constraints
- Mounting/alignment critical; backlash and contact pattern sensitive.
- Hypoid needs viscous HP oil.
- Manufacturing more complex than spur.

## Failure modes
- Edge contact/pitting from misalignment.
- Wear and noise if contact pattern off.
- Lubrication failure in hypoid.

## When to use
Right-angle drives, differentials, hand drills, mixers, miter drives.

## DFM (small workshop)
Prefer straight bevel for low speed; use off-the-shelf sets; check contact pattern with marking compound.
