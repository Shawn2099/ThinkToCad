# clock_spring

**Type:** spring
**Function:** Store rotational energy over multiple turns using a flat strip wound in a spiral.

## How it works
A thin flat strip is wound into a spiral; one end anchors to an arbour, the other to a housing. Rotation winds or unwinds the strip, storing energy; output torque varies with number of turns.

## Key dimensions / equations
- Torque T ~ E b t^3 / (12 R) (approx, per turn geometry); exact per spring design.
- Stored energy scales with number of turns and strip length.
- Fatigue life depends on bending strain in strip.

## Constraints
- Output torque changes with wind/unwind state.
- Requires guiding to prevent strip buckling/tangling.
- Limited by fatigue in thin strip.

## Failure modes
- Strip fatigue or fracture.
- Tangling/buckling of coils.
- Loss of preload/anchoring.

## When to use
Steering-wheel clock springs (airbag/electrical continuity), seat mechanisms, medical/aerospace rotary energy storage.

## DFM (small workshop)
Use pre-wound commercial clock springs; design arbours/housings with smooth edges and retain ends securely.
